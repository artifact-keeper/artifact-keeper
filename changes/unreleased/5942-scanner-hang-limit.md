- **A scanner that never returns no longer leaves its scan `running` for half an hour** (#5942). A queued scan job (`SCAN_QUEUE_ENABLED`) that ran out of its 600 s budget was dropped whole: the hung scanner's `scan_results` row (and on an on-demand scan the rows of the scanners after it) stayed `running` until the stuck-scan janitor failed them after `STUCK_SCAN_THRESHOLD_SECS`. Each scanner in a queued job now stops when the budget runs out, its row is recorded `failed`, and the job is retried. The scanners of one job share the budget, so two hung scanners no longer use it up one after the other. A retry of an on-demand scan reuses the scan's row: it goes back to `running` and, when the retry succeeds, ends `completed` without the earlier attempt's error. Between attempts the row reads `failed`, so a `block_on_fail` scan policy holds the artifact until a retry succeeds, which releases the hold. This replaces the 1.12.2 attempt (#6100), whose fixed 300 s limit cut off slow scans that had been finishing and never retried them.

  The new `SCANNER_PROCESS_TIMEOUT_SECS` limits each local `grype` or `trivy` process, in queued and in-process scans. Past it the process is killed and its row recorded `failed`, and a queued job is retried. It is unset by default, so nothing that finishes today is cut short. Scans through the scanner adapter keep the adapter's own budgets. On a pod that scans without the queue (the default), the stuck-scan janitor marks a hung scan's rows `failed` but never stops the hung `grype`, which keeps its CPU and memory. Set `SCANNER_PROCESS_TIMEOUT_SECS=1800`, the janitor's default threshold, to have the process killed when its row is failed, or a lower value (for example `900`) if your slowest scans finish well inside it.
