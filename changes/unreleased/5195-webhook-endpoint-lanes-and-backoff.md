---
section: Changed
issues: [#5195]
---
- **A failing or unreachable webhook endpoint no longer delays deliveries to other webhooks, and new deliveries go out within seconds instead of on the next 30-second tick** (#5195). The retry worker claimed every 30 seconds and waited for its slowest send. A receiver that timed out (30 s) therefore held every other webhook's new delivery until the pass ended and the next tick came, typically 25-30 s and up to a minute. A receiver that was down was also sent one delivery on every pass. Now:

  - **Wake on enqueue, never wait for a send.** The producer wakes the delivery worker as soon as it has queued a delivery, and each webhook gets its own lane task (up to 8 per replica, 2 of them always kept for endpoints that are not failing). A finished lane wakes the worker again, so a busy endpoint drains continuously. A hanging receiver holds up only its own lane. When nothing wakes it, the worker polls every 5 seconds for retries that have come due.
  - **One lane per endpoint, across replicas.** A claim takes at most 10 of a webhook's oldest due deliveries. It holds a per-webhook advisory lock and re-checks in-flight state after taking it, so two replicas never send to one endpoint at once and each endpoint's deliveries go out in order. `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and the claim token still guarantee that each delivery is sent by one owner. The claim reads pending rows per webhook through a new partial index, `idx_webhook_deliveries_pending_by_webhook` (migration 417, built `CONCURRENTLY`). It no longer ranks every due row in the table.
  - **Per-endpoint backoff.** After a send that says the endpoint is unavailable, the endpoint backs off as a whole: no response, 3xx, 5xx, or a 4xx other than 400/409/413/415/422. Its other deliveries are released unattempted and keep their attempt counts, and the worker skips the webhook for 30 s, 60 s, 120 s, then 300 s per further consecutive failure. No step is shorter than the poll interval. A payload rejection (400, 409, 413, 415, 422) reschedules only that delivery and does not back off the endpoint. If the endpoint answered earlier in the same lane, the count restarts at 1. A delivered send or a payload rejection clears the backoff, and so does re-enabling the webhook. Each delivery keeps its own retry schedule and `max_attempts` as before. A receiver that is down is now probed about 12 times an hour instead of about 120, and a receiver that recovers gets its queue within 5 minutes. The state is held in two new `webhooks` columns, `delivery_failures` and `delivery_backoff_until` (migration 416, additive).
  - **Metrics.** New: `ak_webhook_endpoint_failures_total{webhook_id}`, `ak_webhook_endpoint_backoff_seconds{webhook_id}`, and `ak_webhook_delivery_backlog{webhook_id}`, the pending deliveries per webhook, refreshed every minute. See `docs/operations/webhook-delivery.md`.

  **Default changes and how to keep the old behaviour.** `WEBHOOK_DELIVERY_POLL_SECS` (default `5`) sets the idle poll interval and the shortest endpoint backoff. `WEBHOOK_ENDPOINT_BACKOFF_MAX_SECS` (default `300`) caps the endpoint backoff. `0` leaves only the poll-interval floor, one attempt per poll for a failing endpoint. For the previous cadence, set `WEBHOOK_DELIVERY_POLL_SECS=30` and `WEBHOOK_ENDPOINT_BACKOFF_MAX_SECS=0`: a failing endpoint is then tried once every 30 s, as before. New deliveries are still sent as soon as they are enqueued. Because a down endpoint is probed less often by default, its deliveries use up their attempts more slowly, and the auto-disable that follows a dead letter comes later than before.
