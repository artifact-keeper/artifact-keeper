---
section: Changed
issues: [#5195]
---
- **A failing or unreachable webhook endpoint no longer delays deliveries to other webhooks, and new deliveries go out within seconds instead of on the next 30-second tick** (#5195). The retry worker claimed every 30 seconds and waited for its slowest send, so a receiver that timed out (30 s) held every other webhook's new delivery until the pass ended and the next tick came, typically 25-30 s and up to a minute. A receiver that was down was also sent one delivery on every pass. Now:

  - **Wake on enqueue, never wait for a send.** The producer wakes the delivery worker as soon as it has queued a delivery, and the worker gives each webhook its own lane task. A finished lane wakes the worker again, so a busy endpoint drains continuously. A hanging receiver holds up only its own lane. When nothing wakes it, the worker polls every 5 seconds for retries that have come due.
  - **One lane per endpoint, a bounded claim.** Each claim takes at most 10 of a webhook's oldest due deliveries and skips a webhook that already has a delivery in flight on any replica. Each endpoint's deliveries therefore go out one at a time in `next_retry_at` order, and two replicas never send to the same endpoint at once. `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and the claim token still guarantee that each delivery is sent by one owner. The claim reads each webhook's pending rows through a new partial index, `idx_webhook_deliveries_pending_by_webhook` (migration 417, built `CONCURRENTLY`). It no longer ranks every due row in the table.
  - **Per-endpoint backoff.** A failed send backs off the whole endpoint, not only that delivery. The endpoint's other deliveries are released unattempted, keeping their attempt counts, and the worker skips the webhook for 30 s, 60 s, 120 s, then 300 s per further consecutive failure. A successful send, or re-enabling the webhook, clears the backoff. Each delivery keeps its own retry schedule and `max_attempts` as before. A receiver that is down is now probed about 12 times an hour instead of about 120, and a receiver that recovers gets its queue within 5 minutes. The state is held in two new `webhooks` columns, `delivery_failures` and `delivery_backoff_until` (migration 416, additive).
  - **Metrics.** New: `ak_webhook_endpoint_failures_total{webhook_id}`, `ak_webhook_endpoint_backoff_seconds{webhook_id}`, and `ak_webhook_delivery_backlog{webhook_id}`, the pending deliveries per webhook, refreshed every minute.

  **Default changes and how to keep the old behaviour.** `WEBHOOK_DELIVERY_POLL_SECS` (default `5`) sets the idle poll interval. `WEBHOOK_ENDPOINT_BACKOFF_MAX_SECS` (default `300`) caps the endpoint backoff, and `0` turns endpoint backoff off. For the previous cadence, set `WEBHOOK_DELIVERY_POLL_SECS=30` and `WEBHOOK_ENDPOINT_BACKOFF_MAX_SECS=0`. Deliveries are still woken on enqueue. Because a down endpoint is probed less often, its deliveries use up their attempts more slowly, and the auto-disable that follows a dead letter comes later than before.
